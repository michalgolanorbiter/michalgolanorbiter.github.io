RESEARCH

Research

Collisionless Shocks

Internal shock structure, transition regions, and downstream plasma dynamics.

Magnetic Structure

Coherent magnetic rotations, fluctuations, and organization in quasi-parallel shock environments.

Heliospheric Observations

Comparative analysis of planetary and interplanetary shocks using in-situ spacecraft measurements.

Collisionless shocks are fundamental plasma structures found throughout space and astrophysical environments. In these systems, electromagnetic fields and collective plasma processes mediate the conversion of directed flow energy into heat, magnetic energy, and energetic particles.

My research investigates the internal organization of collisionless shocks and the plasma structures that develop across their transition regions and downstream environments.

A central focus of my current work is the magnetic structure of quasi-parallel shocks. These shocks are often described as highly turbulent and irregular, yet spacecraft observations can reveal surprisingly coherent magnetic-field rotations within the shock transition.

I use observations from multiple spacecraft missions and heliospheric environments to determine how universal these structures are, how their spatial and temporal scales compare between shocks, and what they reveal about collisionless shock formation and relaxation.
